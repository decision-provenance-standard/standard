| `decision_statement` | The disclosure block on Mode 2 content, and on AI-drafted content embedded in Mode 1 artifacts, is the Standard's own requirement, and whether Article 50 of the EU AI Act, or any other disclosure law, applies to an artifact is for the deployer to determine. A Charter may declare outputs outside that requirement only by rev. 8's binding output-based test, where the deployer has verified that those outputs are produced outside the European Union and will not reach, and are not reasonably foreseeable to reach, people in the European Union, and a Charter written before v1.1 (reading edition rev. 9) whose outputs already met that test remains valid without the declaration; `declaring-authority` names the person who prepares the disclosure and the organization they act for, a record that names only the person, or only the organization, remains valid, and new records SHOULD name both. Disclosure-review records are required (MUST) for Charters declared `mode-2` or `mode-1-with-embedded-mode-2-summary`, which rev. 8's "when applicable per dispatch mode" already required and v1.1 states explicitly, and recommended (SHOULD) for a `mode-1` Charter whose records embed AI-drafted content, with none needed for outputs a Charter declares outside the requirement or that the transition sentence keeps outside it; the per-level tables also list the six lifecycle signals that §7.1 already assigns to Levels 2 and 3, with no signal's meaning or level changed. |
| `context_at_decision` | • Rev. 8 stated when Article 50 applies, including its reach outside the EU, and headed §4.6 "Article 50 Conformance".<br>• Rev. 8 stated the exclusion two ways: its binding test is about outputs (produced within the EU, or reaching or reasonably foreseeable to reach EU natural persons), while its exclusion sentence also spoke of deployers "operating exclusively outside the EU".<br>• Rev. 8 said both that the declaring authority is a person, since the disclosure obligation "belongs to a person" (§2.2.12), and that it is "the legal entity" (§4.6.2), so records written to either section name only a person or only an organization.<br>• Rev. 8 scheduled disclosure-review records "when applicable per dispatch mode" (§7.2.1) and "when applicable" (§6.3.1), without spelling out which modes, and listed six signals only outside the per-level tables.<br>• A standard that says it is not legal advice should not state what a law requires. |
| `options_considered` | • Keep the rev. 8 text. Considered because it changes nothing. Rejected: the text would go on stating what a law requires.<br>• Remove the disclosure block. Considered because the block was framed around one law. Rejected: it removes a required field and a Level 2 signal, which is a major change.<br>• Keep the block as the Standard's own requirement, state the exclusion with rev. 8's binding output-based test, and leave legal applicability to the deployer. Chosen: it removes the legal claims, and no record or Charter valid under rev. 8 becomes invalid.<br>• Keep rev. 8's exclusion sentence limit "operating exclusively outside the EU". Considered because rev. 8's exclusion sentence used it. Rejected: it conflicts with rev. 8's binding output-based test, so it would narrow the rule for some deployers.<br>• Make the block universal, with no exclusion. Considered as the simplest rule. Rejected: it makes records valid under rev. 8 invalid, which is a major change.<br>• Open the exclusion to any Charter. Considered because it would drop the verification test. Rejected: it changes what Level 2 means in practice.<br>• Require new records to name both the person and the organization (MUST). Considered because both are useful to a reader. Rejected: nothing in a record shows which revision it was written under, so the rule could not be checked without making some rev. 8 records invalid; it is a SHOULD instead.<br>• Make disclosure-review records required for every Charter whose records carry a disclosure block. Considered as the strictest reading of "when applicable". Rejected: a `mode-1` Charter that was valid under rev. 8 without them would become invalid, so for that case they are recommended instead. |
