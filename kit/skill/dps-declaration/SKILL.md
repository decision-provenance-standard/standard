# Draft a Decision Provenance Standard self-declaration

A self-declaration is the declarer's own claim about its own records. Nobody grades it: the Standard's Steward does not validate, grade or audit declarations. Your job is to help the people in this repository make an honest claim from real evidence. You draft; a named person at the organization reads, confirms and publishes.

Files you rely on (in `references/`, generated from one source; do not edit them):
- `criteria.md` (and `criteria.json`): every Conformance Level criterion, with its section of the Standard and its signal name; "How to check" (the order); "The rule" (how to work out the Level); "No Charter yet" (what to say when there is none); and the plain-text shapes of the declaration.
- `dps-declaration.schema.json`: the declaration format. Your JSON must match it exactly, including the fixed statement text.

## Rules

- **Evidence only.** Mark a criterion "met" only when you can point to the file (and field or line) that shows it, or the person names where the evidence is kept. Mark it "not met" when the files show it is missing and nobody names another place where it is kept, or when the person is sure it is missing; add a note on what would show it. Mark it "not sure" when you asked and the person does not know. Never assume a fact, and never invent a record, a date, a person or an audit.
- **A fact without evidence is "not sure".** If the person tells you something is true but neither of you can point to where it is written or recorded, record "not sure". "Not sure" does not meet the criterion. The fix list then says what evidence to record, and where.
- **Evidence outside the repository.** Evidence kept elsewhere counts when the person, or a field in the repository, names where it is kept and its reference (for example "kept by HR, reference HR-2026-007"). Mark it "outside the repository" in the report. You need not ask for it when a field already names the place and the reference.
- **A Charter's own claim is not evidence.** If a Charter already states a Level in its own text (for example a `conformance_level_declared` field), note the claim, grade from the evidence anyway, and say plainly in the report if the two differ. When the claim is higher than the Level reached, add to the fix list: lower the Charter's claimed Level to the Level it reaches, or close the gaps between them.
- **You read records; you do not perform the reviews.** Some criteria depend on work a person does (the peer-review sample audit, affirmations). Look for the record of that work. Do not do the audit yourself and count it.
- **Words.** Never describe the organization, the product or the result as certified, compliant, verified or approved. The word is "self-declared".
- **No badge code.** Do not write badge HTML, Markdown or images. When the declaration is ready, point the person to the badge page on the Standard's website, where they paste the JSON: https://decisionprovenancestandard.org/badges.html
- **A person stands behind it.** The name and role of the person who stands behind the declaration come from that person or their organization. Never fill in your own name or a guess.

## When to ask, and when to carry on

- **Ask only for what you need and cannot find in the repository.** That is usually: the organization's name; where the Charters are (if you find none, or cannot tell which files are Charters); where a Charter's records are (if the Charter does not say and you cannot find them); at which levels a Charter's records are (executive, function leader, team leader, or an individual professional's own work), when the records carry no `altitude` field, since L1-01 and L2-09 depend on it; whether to leave out Charters that reach no Level (when some Charters reach a Level and others do not); and, when there is something to declare, the name and role of the person who stands behind it.
- **Skip a question whose answer cannot change the result.** For example, do not ask where a Charter's records are when the Charter already fails Level 1 on its own fields.
- **Ask in as few messages as you can.** If you need an answer before you can check (where the Charters are, or where a Charter's records are), ask it first, together with the organization's name. Otherwise check first, then ask everything in one message. A second message is right when the first answer decides what to ask next: for example, when no Charter is found, first ask where the Charters are; only if there are none, explain and offer the product path (step 3).
- **Always ask for the organization's name, in your first message of questions, even if the check will stop early.** Put it at the top of the evidence report. Do not take it from folder, repository or file names, or from fields inside the records. You may say which name you saw and ask whether to use it.
- **For everything else, carry on** and write each assumption down in the evidence report. For example: which files you treated as Charters, which folder holds a Charter's records, the date you used.

## Steps

1. **Look first.** Search the repository for Charters and decision records in whatever form they take: Markdown, JSON or YAML files, a folder of decision records, a database export, fields such as `mode_declaration`, `accountable_owner`, `dispatch_mode`, `affirmation_record` or `seal_hash`.
2. **Organization or product?** An organization declares a Conformance Level for its own Charters. A product only declares that it implements the Standard; a product never carries a Level or Charters. If what you found makes it clear, say which and why; if not, ask. For a product, skip to step 6.
3. **No Charter found?** Ask where the Charters are (with the organization's name). If there is no Charter, or the person does not know what one is: explain in the two plain sentences under "No Charter yet" in `references/criteria.md`; say that no organization Level can be declared without at least one Charter; offer a product declaration instead, in case they make a product that implements the Standard; and point them to the Standard's section on Charters (the link is in the same place).
   - If they say they make such a product, switch to the product path: go to step 6. A product gets only a declaration file, no evidence report.
   - Otherwise write the short no-Charter evidence report (step 7) and stop. No Level, no declaration.
4. **Check each Charter against `references/criteria.md`, one Level at a time, starting with Level 1.** Check every criterion of that Level, even after one is not met, so the list of gaps is complete. Only if every criterion of the Level is met, go on to the next Level. Stop at the first Level with any criterion not met, and do not check the Levels above it. For every criterion checked, record: met, not met, not sure or does not apply; the evidence (file path, and the field or line); and, where not met, what would fix it. A criterion with "Applies only if" counts as met when its condition is not true for this Charter; say how you know the condition is false. Where a criterion has "How to judge", follow it.
5. **Work out the Level** by "The rule" in `references/criteria.md`. A declaration lists exactly the Charters it covers, and its Level applies to those Charters only: the lowest Level any of them reaches. When some Charters reach a Level and others do not, ask whether to leave out the ones that reach none (with your other questions), or to fix them first and declare later. A Charter that reaches no Level can never be in a Level claim. The person may choose to leave out other Charters too. The report names every Charter left out and gives the reason. If no Charter reaches Level 1, say so plainly, give the fix list, write the evidence report (step 7) and stop: there is nothing to declare yet.
6. **Ask for the remaining facts,** in one message with any other question: the name and role of the person who stands behind the declaration; and, optionally, the https:// page where it will be published. For a product, also its name. Use today's date as the date of the declaration unless you are told another, and say so.
7. **Write the files** in a `decision-provenance/` folder at the root of the repository, unless the person named another place. Say where you wrote them.
   - **For an organization, always write `evidence-report-<date>.md`, even when you stopped early.** It starts with the organization's name, as the person gave it. It says: what you checked (which files and folders, which Levels); what you found and where (file path, field or line); one row per criterion checked, per Charter (ID, result, evidence, what to fix); the Level each Charter reached and the Level where it stopped; any Charter left out, and why; any Level a Charter claims in its own text, and whether the evidence bears it out; and every assumption you made.
   - **When there is no Charter, the report is short:** the organization's name; the date of the report (today's date, which also names the file); what you searched and what you found (file paths); that no Charter was found and what the person said; the two sentences from "No Charter yet" and the link to the Standard's section on Charters; and your assumptions. No criteria table.
   - **Only when there is something to declare, also write `declaration-<date>.json`**, matching `references/dps-declaration.schema.json` exactly. Copy the statement for its kind word for word from the schema: the first statement for that kind, unless the Standard's Steward is the organization declaring, or made the product; then the second. List exactly the Charters the declaration covers, each as "Name (ID)" from its `charter_name` and `charter_id`. Leave `declaration_page` out if none was given. Use `standard_version` "v1.2-rev10". Draft against v1.2 (reading edition rev. 10) only; a Charter that meets v1.1 also meets v1.2, which is a minor release. Check the file against the schema before you finish: paste it into the badge page (https://decisionprovenancestandard.org/badges.html), which checks it and explains any problem, or run `python -m jsonschema -i decision-provenance/declaration-<date>.json <this skill's folder>/references/dps-declaration.schema.json` (needs the Python `jsonschema` package; exit code 0 means the file is valid, even if the command prints a warning).
   - **For a product, write only `declaration-<date>.json`.** A product is not checked against the Level criteria, so it has no evidence report.
8. **Hand it over.**
   - When there is something to declare: show the plain-text declaration, in the exact shape given in `references/criteria.md` (with no publication page, leave out the "Where this declaration can be read" line), then say that this is the declarer's own claim and the named person should read and confirm it before it is published; and that to get the badge, they paste the JSON into https://decisionprovenancestandard.org/badges.html. For an organization only, also say that the Standard asks the organization to keep the declaration in its own decision register (§7.1).
   - When you stopped with nothing to declare: say so plainly, say where the evidence report is, and list the fixes for the Level where each Charter stopped.
